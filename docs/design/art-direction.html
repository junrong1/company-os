Company OS · Art Direction · 2026-08-12

A lit office on ink ground.

The office glows in traditional pigment colour; the console around it stays ink. Smallville's information design comes across — status glyphs above the head, click to inspect, knowledge that is partial by default — but not its daylight. A night-shift control room reads as consequence. A sunny village reads as play, and play is the framing this product rejected.

01 / STAGE

The anchor

Two rooms in pigment on ink ground, drawn through the same runtime ASCII-grid pipeline the prototype already uses. One person is working and carries a status glyph at the shoulder. One is waiting on a decision and carries the beam above the head. The two signals never occupy the same space, which is what keeps the beam readable at a glance.

02 / SEMANTIC SLOTS

Chrome keeps its meanings, swaps its hues

Every slot below already existed. Only the hue is re-sourced. The beam is the exception and stays exactly as it was: it is the product's single reserved signal, it is already tuned, and re-sourcing everything around it leaves it the most saturated warm in the frame.

04 / LOAD

The ramp must not pass through yellow

Capacity runs green to red. The obvious interpolation goes through yellow, and yellow is the beam. A department at two-thirds load would then look like a person waiting on a decision, and the product's one reserved signal would be spent on ambient telemetry. The ramp desaturates through 苍绿 instead.

05 / GEOMETRY

Beam above the head, glyph at the shoulder

Smallville puts an emoji speech bubble directly above the avatar. That space is already spoken for here, so the status glyph moves down and to the side. Both signals can be present at once without competing.

Beam. A vertical column rising from directly above the head, 8 px wide at 1×, fading to transparent. Breathes at 1.6 s. The only indefinite loop in the product.

Status glyph. An 8×8 pixel glyph in a plate offset 6 px right and 4 px down from the head top, drawn through the sprite pipeline rather than as emoji. System emoji would be the wrong resolution, would drift between platforms, and would be the one thing on the canvas not made of pixels.

Inspect shows less than conversation. Clicking a person reveals observable state only — their item, effort burned, load, what they are blocked on. The tacit line stays earned by walking over. This is the one place where adopting Smallville's affordance wholesale would dissolve the mechanic the product is built on.

06 / GLYPHS

Status vocabulary

Eight states, each an 8×8 ASCII grid compiled at runtime, validated for rectangularity the way test/sprites.js validates the character sheets. Shown at 4× and 2×.

07 / LABELS

A 5×7 bitmap face, generated not downloaded

Room names and person labels are currently drawn with the system sans over the canvas, which floats on top of the art instead of belonging to it. A 5×7 bitmap alphabet built from the same grid pipeline stays crisp at every integer zoom, needs no network request, and reads as part of the world. The prototype's zero-request property survives.

08 / DAG

Circuit board, not flowchart

The failure mode for this view is a default graph library: bezier curves, drop shadows, rounded pills. Drawn instead on the same 16 px grid as the office, with orthogonally routed pixel-stepped edges, it belongs to the same world. A node carries a department stripe in its floor colour and a load tint, so an overloaded path is visible without opening the office.

09 / MOTION

Three motions, no more

Beam breathing

1600ms · ease-in-out · infinite

Waiting has to read as waiting, not as a static badge. The only loop allowed to run forever.

Load bleed

800ms · ease-in-out · on change

Capacity slides across a room floor rather than snapping. A soft ceiling should feel soft.

Unlock cascade

160ms stagger · on completion

Edges draw forward and downstream nodes light in dependency order. Carries information.

The walk cycle stays as it is — four facings, three frames, already built and already tested. Everything here honours prefers-reduced-motion; the beam falls back to its full-opacity state so the signal never disappears.
